I work with e-editiones and TEI Publisher, as a developer and research software engineer, based in Europe. I have served on the TEI Technical Council and I currently serve on the TEI Board of Directors.

## How I first got involved

In the early years of the new millennium I was asked to develop a workflow for a parallel edition of the Polish-Hispanic *Corpus Diplomaticum* at the University of Warsaw. In the course of that work — and in my ignorance — what I developed was a poor-woman’s version of TEI. I only learned about the real thing a couple of years later, just a tip from a colleague in Gent about TEI and the DALF (Digital Archive of Letters in Flanders). At the time it was one of the rare treasures: a project openly sharing their encoding guidelines online.

What followed was the application of the TEI Guidelines to the Corpus of Texts and Correspondence of Ioannes Dantiscus, where I was charged to define a data model and encoding scheme — and design a curation and publication system for it.

And, two decades later, this is still my daily bread and butter.

## Working as part of the Consortium

I threw my hat in the ring in 2015, standing for election to the TEI Technical Council, and consequently served on the Council for a number of terms from January 2016 to December 2024. For the term 2024–2026 I have been elected a member of the TEI Board of Directors, and in 2026 appointed Secretary. Before my formal engagement with TEI-C I worked on the TEI Simple project, in which the Consortium partnered, alongside the University of Oxford (where I was employed at the time), Northwestern, and Nebraska-Lincoln.

At the time I already had several years of experience using the TEI, but for the first time I could benefit from the critical review and exchange of ideas with other members of the community. I have been lucky indeed to have figures like Lou Burnard (one of the first editors of the TEI Guidelines), Sebastian Rahtz (primary author of the TEI Stylesheets), Elena Pierazzo and Martin Mueller (Board), and James Cummings (Chair of the Technical Council at the time) as my mentors and sparring partners. I definitely consider these years a formative period of my professional development, which allowed me to dedicate myself in full to open source software development and community-oriented work. Engagement in the TEI Council only reinforced and supported that commitment, with continued interaction with the people devoted to the maintenance of the standard and to community service.

## Passing on TEI knowledge

TEI very early on gave me a language to express my data models in a vocabulary that can be understood world-wide. With that, I have never looked back. Obviously, I have been passing my understanding of the TEI to others — in workshops, teaching undergraduates as part of the University curriculum, but even more often through discussions regarding concrete projects. Particularly the latter is for me a quite delightful puzzle to solve, even if it comes with a need to “pitch” the solution to the editors. This already feels like a reward, but even greater is to enjoy a project going live. In my case, I do get a bit of a kick walking into a room where every screen is lit up with an interface I built, editors buried in their benedictine work of enriching their material with TEI.

## Why become a TEI-C member?

In the past, the Consortium has been primarily funded by a relatively small number of institutional members contributing at the highest membership level. This justly reflected their ongoing commitment to preserving our shared cultural heritage in an open, machine-processable format. Unfortunately, our institutional membership rates dwindle, and we all need to step up if we want to support our community and uphold its guiding principles. What it means in particular is to use any influence we have to increase the institutional membership rates, starting with our own institutions. In the short term, it might also mean supporting the Consortium directly, with individual membership. We all know that an individual voice can be easily dismissed. TEI has 40 years of history and gives a platform to hundreds and hundreds of people. It should not end with us.
