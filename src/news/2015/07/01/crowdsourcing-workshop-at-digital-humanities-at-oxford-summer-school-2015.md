**Crowdsourcing for Academic, Library and Museum Environments –**<br/>**  Citizen Science for the Digital Humanities**


<http://dhoxss.humanities.ox.ac.uk/2015/crowdsourcing.html>


– Taught by experts from Google and Zooniverse (Oxford’s Citizen<br/>
Science platform).


– How to plan your crowdsourcing all the way from project<br/>
conception to launch to data analysis.


– In-depth experience of all stages of Zooniverse’s new DIY<br/>
crowdsourcing site from developing and launching a beta site<br/>
through to using data refinement and analysis tools to understand<br/>
the results.


– Come with your own project ideas and set of images to work from<br/>
to get practical experience in building a project with a dataset<br/>
that you understand.


– Opportunity to pitch your project, generate interest in it,<br/>
attract a crowd from amongst Summer School delegates, and get<br/>
them to respond to it. There will be time to reflect on the<br/>
process of setting up and sustaining a crowdsourcing project.<br/><br/>
This workshop is run as part of the Digital Humanities at Oxford<br/>
Summer School, 20 – 24 July 2015. For more information see:<br/><http://dhoxss.humanities.ox.ac.uk/2015/ml/>


Other workshops include:<br/>
– An Introduction to Digital Humanities<br/>
– Crowdsourcing for Academic, Library and Museum Environments<br/>
– Digital Approaches in Medieval and Renaissance Studies<br/>
– Digital Musicology<br/>
– From Text to Tech<br/>
– Humanities Data: Curation, Analysis, Access, and Reuse<br/>
– Leveraging the Text Encoding Initiative<br/>
– Linked Data for the Humanities


Keynote Speakers:<br/>
– Jane Winters, Institute of Historical Research, University of<br/>
London<br/>
– James Loxley, University of Edinburgh


Additional Lectures:<br/>
Supplement your chosen workshop with a choice from 9 additional<br/>
morning sessions covering a variety of Digital Humanities topics.


Evening Events:<br/>
Join us for events every evening, include a research poster and<br/>
drinks reception, guided walking tour of Oxford, the annual TORCH<br/>
Digital Humanities lecture, and a dinner at Exeter College.
