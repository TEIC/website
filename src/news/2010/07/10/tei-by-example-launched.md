We’re very pleased to announce the completion and launch of TEI by<br/>
Example: <http://www.teibyexample.org>.


TEI By Example (TBE) offers a series of freely available online tutorials<br/>
walking individuals through the different stages in marking up a document<br/>
in [TEI (Text Encoding Initiative)](/). Besides a general introduction to text<br/>
encoding, step-by-step tutorial modules provide example-based<br/>
introductions to eight different aspects of electronic text markup for the<br/>
humanities. Each tutorial module is accompanied with a dedicated examples<br/>
section, illustrating actual TEI encoding practise with real-life<br/>
examples. The theory of the tutorial modules can be tested in interactive<br/>
tests and exercises. The tutorial materials are contextualised with a TBE<br/>
validator application, allowing you to test your TEI encoding as you type!


We hope you will consider using TEI by Example in your (online)teaching<br/>
and refer students of markup to these tutorials.<br/>
We also hope you will submit more examples of encoding for inclusion in TBE.


We’re eager to receive your comments and learn about your use of TEI by<br/>
example in (self-)teaching environments.


Please contact the editorial team with any feedback at [teibyexample@kantl.be](mailto:).


Funding for the project has been made available by the Association of<br/>
Literary and Linguistic Computing, the Centre for Computers and the<br/>
Humanities – King’s College London, UCL Centre for Digital Humanities, and<br/>
the Centre for Scholarly Editing and Document Studies of the Royal Academy<br/>
of Dutch Language and Literature.
