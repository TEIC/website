Dear Colleague,


This is a first call for submissions of papers and posters for the 2014<br/>
TEI meeting, which will be held at Northwestern University, October 22-24,<br/>
2014. Northwestern is located in Evanston, a northern suburb of Chicago<br/>
with excellent public transportation to Chicago’s “Loop.”


 The deadline for submissions will be April 30, 2014. More soon about the<br/>
details of the submission process. As in previous years, we welcome<br/>
submissions on anything plausibly related to the Text Encoding Initiative,<br/>
but we have a special interest in the following topics:


1.  The TEI is about text “encoding,” but encoded texts need to be<br/>
“decoded” by readers who put the encoding to various uses, increasingly<br/>
with the aid of digital tools of one kind or another.  What is the<br/>
scholarly value added by encoding? What can people do with TEI encoded<br/>
texts (and what have they done) that they could not otherwise do or have<br/>
done?


2.  In 2015 some 25,000 TEI-encoded Text Creation Partnership (TCP) texts<br/>
printed before 1700 will be released into the public domain, and another<br/>
45,000 texts will be released in the five years to follow, producing by<br/>
2020 a deduplicated, structurally encoded, and open source library of just<br/>
about every English book printed before 1800.  This is a very<br/>
consequential event for the documentary infrastructure of Early Modern<br/>
Studies in the Anglophone world. It is also an important event for the<br/>
TEI. 


3. As announced earlier, the TEI meeting will partly overlap and share<br/>
some programming with the Chicago Digital Humanities and Computer Science<br/>
Colloquium, a lively regional conference. Music will be one of its<br/>
featured topics and may be a good topic for shared programming. We will<br/>
welcome submissions about TEI and MEI in the context of music in a digital<br/>
world. 


We have not yet decided on keynote speakers, and I would very much welcome<br/>
off-line suggestions about suitable candidates.


With best wishes


Martin Mueller<br/>
Chair, Programming Committee, TEI Conference 2014<br/>
Professor emeritus of English and Classics<br/>
Northwestern University
